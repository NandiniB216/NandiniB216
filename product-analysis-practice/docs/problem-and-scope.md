# Problem, Scope and Assumptions

## 1. Problem statement

People who want to complete a short guided movement session may need to understand the session length, space required, equipment, instructions and completion steps before starting. A poorly explained flow can create uncertainty and abandonment.

**Proposed product opportunity:** make session choice and completion easier to understand through clear session information, readiness guidance and visible progress.

This is a fictional scenario for skills demonstration—not a finding from user research.

## 2. Users

| Illustrative user | Need |
|---|---|
| Desk-based adult | Understand whether a short session can be completed beside a desk |
| Home user | Know whether floor space or equipment is required |
| First-time user | Understand the steps before starting |
| Returning user | Resume or select another session without confusion |

These are hypothetical user types. They must be validated through research before product decisions are finalised.

## 3. Goals

- Help users choose a session with enough information.
- Explain preparation requirements before the session begins.
- Make progress and completion states understandable.
- Provide clear ways to pause, exit or recover from interruptions.

## 4. Out of scope

- Diagnosis, treatment or personalised medical advice
- Clinical claims or recovery outcomes
- Automated assessment of exercise form
- Camera-based pose estimation or repetition counting
- Integration with wearables or medical records
- A production-ready mobile or web application

## 5. Assumptions to validate

1. Users want to know session duration and space needs before starting.
2. Clear preparation instructions reduce avoidable exits.
3. Users understand the difference between paused, interrupted and completed.
4. Users can complete the proposed flow using accessible instructions.

## 6. Risks and safeguards

- **Safety:** avoid implying that a generic session is suitable for everyone; route health concerns to qualified professionals.
- **Privacy:** do not request camera or other device permissions unless a real feature needs them and the reason is explained.
- **Accessibility:** use plain language, readable contrast, keyboard-accessible controls and text alternatives.
- **Measurement:** treat completion rate as a product signal, not proof of health benefit.

## 7. Success measures (proposed)

- Session selection completion rate
- Readiness-step completion rate
- Session start-to-completion rate
- Pause and exit rates
- Help/instruction views

No baseline or target is claimed because no real product data exists.
